Into Poldark Country

The wild Tin Coast of West Cornwall — where the 18th-century world of Ross Poldark was brought to life. Dramatic clifftop mines, crashing Atlantic waves, and paths along the edge of England.

Botallack Mine & Crown Mines

"Wheal Leisure" in Poldark

The most iconic image from the show: two engine houses perched impossibly on the cliffs above the Atlantic. This is where Ross's mining adventures come alive. A 15-minute walk from the car park along windswept coastal paths leads to the clifftop — and views that will take your breath away. The mines were real, dating to the 1830s, and the crashing waves below are as dramatic in person as they are on screen.

Levant Mine & Beam Engine

Working Victorian mine

Just up the coast from Botallack, Levant is home to the oldest working beam engine in Cornwall — a giant Victorian machine that once pumped water from the mine shafts deep under the sea. Walk the South West Coast Path between Botallack and Levant (about a mile along the cliffs) for one of the most stunning coastal walks in Britain. Wild, windswept, and exactly what Poldark fans picture.

St Michael's Mount

Tidal island castle

A fairy-tale castle on a tidal island, rising from Mount's Bay just off the coast at Marazion. At low tide you walk across the ancient cobbled causeway; at high tide, you take a boat. The castle and subtropical gardens have been home to the St Aubyn family for generations. Visible from the Penzance promenade, it's the kind of place that makes you understand why Cornwall has inspired storytellers for centuries.

The Cornish Experience

Cream teas, seafood & pasties

Cornwall isn't just filming locations — it's a world unto itself. Cream teas in a harbour cafe (cream first, then jam — the Cornish way). Fresh-caught fish and chips on the seafront at sunset. Proper Cornish pasties from a bakery in a harbour town. Penzance's Chapel Street with its ancient pubs, including The Turk's Head — serving since 1233. This is England at its most characterful.

"I am Ross Poldark, and this is my land." — The Tin Coast stretches before you, exactly as it did in the show. No green screen. All real.

Into Outlander Country

The castles, villages, and landscapes of 18th-century Scotland — all within reach of Edinburgh. Step through the stones and into the world of Claire and Jamie Fraser.

Doune Castle

Castle Leoch — Clan MacKenzie

The great hall where Claire first meets Colum MacKenzie. The courtyard where she tends to Jamie's wounds. Doune Castle is the real Castle Leoch — a beautifully preserved medieval fortress near Stirling. The audio guide is narrated by Sam Heughan himself, walking you through exactly where key scenes were filmed. Standing in the great hall, you can almost hear the pipes and feel the firelight. Also famous for Monty Python and the Holy Grail.

Midhope Castle

Lallybroch — Jamie's home

The exterior of Jamie Fraser's beloved family estate — the place he fights to protect, the home he carries in his heart across oceans and centuries. Midhope Castle sits on the Hopetoun Estate just outside Edinburgh. The Z-plan tower house looks exactly as it does on screen: the arched doorway, the courtyard, the tower. Exterior access only (the inside is derelict), but stepping into that courtyard is a genuine goosebumps moment for any fan.

Culross Village

Cranesmuir — Geillis Duncan's village

The beautifully preserved 16th-century village of Culross (pronounced "COO-riss") doubles as Cranesmuir in the show. Walk the cobbled streets past the Mercat Cross where villagers gathered. Visit Culross Palace, whose garden became Claire's herb garden at Castle Leoch. See The Study — Geillis Duncan's house, where Claire first senses something isn't quite right about her new friend. The entire village feels like stepping back in time.

Blackness Castle

Fort William — Black Jack Randall

The imposing ship-shaped fortress on the Firth of Forth that serves as the dreaded Fort William — where Jamie endures his brutal flogging at the hands of Captain "Black Jack" Randall. The 15th-century castle juts into the water like the prow of a stone ship. Dark, atmospheric, and unforgettable. The walls and dungeons feel exactly as oppressive as the show portrays. A compact visit (~45 minutes) that packs an emotional punch.

Falkland Village

1940s Inverness — Where it all begins

The very first scenes of Outlander: Claire and Frank walking the streets of Inverness in 1945. The quaint village of Falkland stands in for the Highland capital. See the Bruce Fountain where Frank glimpses Jamie's ghost staring up at Claire's window — the moment that sets the entire story in motion. The Covenanter Hotel doubles as Mrs Baird's B&B. Walk the same streets Claire walked before her world changed forever.

Edinburgh Itself

The Royal Mile, whisky & history

Edinburgh needs no fictional overlay to be magical. The castle towering over the city. The medieval Royal Mile descending through closes and courtyards. Arthur's Seat — the ancient volcano you can climb for 360-degree views. Ghost tours through underground vaults. A dram of single malt at a Royal Mile whisky bar. Late May means sunset at 9:30 PM and the city bathed in golden light. Edinburgh is where history, drama, and beauty collide.

"You are my home now." — From the standing stones to castle halls, Scotland's Outlander country is as wild and romantic as the story itself.

Board the GWR from Paddington to Penzance — a 5-hour journey through gorgeous countryside. The train hugs the sea wall at Dawlish (sit on the left!). Arrive mid-afternoon, explore Penzance Promenade with views of St Michael's Mount. Dinner at a harbourside restaurant.

Day 4 — Tue, May 26

Poldark Day: Botallack & the Tin Coast

The dramatic clifftop engine houses of Botallack Mine, Levant's working beam engine, and the South West Coast Path between them. Picnic on the cliffs. Afternoon at St Michael's Mount or Holywell Bay — where Ross gallops his horse. Fish and chips as the sun sets over Penzance.

London
Day 5 — Wed, May 27

Cornwall to London → The Play ✓

Early morning train from Penzance, arriving London by 1 PM. Freshen up, then pre-theatre dinner at the National Theatre. Curtain up at 7 PM — Aidan Turner and Lesley Manville live on stage. Post-show drinks on the Thames terrace. A perfect farewell to London.

🎭
Scotland
Day 6 — Thu, May 28

Fly to Edinburgh

Morning flight from London to Edinburgh (~1.5 hours). Check into our Old Town hotel. Afternoon exploring the Royal Mile, Greyfriars Kirkyard, and Calton Hill for panoramic views. Evening in the Grassmarket — pubs, haggis, and your first dram of Scottish whisky.

✈️
Scotland
Day 7 — Fri, May 29

Outlander Day

A full day in Outlander country. Visit Doune Castle (Castle Leoch), Midhope Castle (Lallybroch), Culross (Cranesmuir), Blackness Castle (Fort William), and Falkland (1940s Inverness). Sam Heughan narrates the Doune Castle audio guide. Back to Edinburgh for a celebratory dinner.

🏰
Scotland
Day 8 — Sat, May 30

Edinburgh at Leisure

A free day to explore. Edinburgh Castle and Scotland's crown jewels. Climb Arthur's Seat for 360-degree views. A whisky tasting on the Royal Mile. Dean Village, the Scottish National Gallery, or just wandering the medieval closes. Farewell dinner at a candlelit Edinburgh restaurant.

Leisurely Edinburgh morning. One last walk down the Royal Mile, last-minute whisky and shortbread. Fly home from Edinburgh via London, Amsterdam, or Dublin. Farewell, Britain.
